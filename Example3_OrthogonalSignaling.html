Example 3

Proving Binary Orthogonal Signaling & Vector Representation

The Problem

Goal

  • Prove orthogonality and equal energy for $s'_1(t)$ and $s'_2(t)$.
  • Calculate their geometric vector coordinates using $\psi_1(t)$ and $\psi_2(t)$.

The Signals

  • $s'_1(t) = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}$
  • $s'_2(t) = \pm \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}$ (Flips at $T_b/2$)

The Basis Functions

  • $\psi_1(t)$ ONLY from $0 \to T_b/2$
  • $\psi_2(t)$ ONLY from $T_b/2 \to T_b$

(Pro-Tip: These basis functions are strictly assumed/given by the problem statement. You do not need to manually derive them using Gram-Schmidt!)

The Solution

Part 1: Orthogonality

The orthogonality condition requires the inner product to be zero. Because $s'_2(t)$ flips from positive to negative exactly halfway through the bit period, the integral naturally cancels itself out.

$$ \begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{T_b} s'_1(t)s'_2(t)dt &= \int_{0}^{T_b/2} \left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right) dt + \int_{T_b/2}^{T_b} \left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right)\left(-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right) dt \\[15pt] &= \int_{0}^{T_b/2} \frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b} dt - \int_{T_b/2}^{T_b} \frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b} dt \\[15pt] &= \left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}\right) - \left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}\right) = 0 \end{aligned} $$
Part 2: Equal Energy

Energy is defined as the integral of the squared signal. Squaring removes the negative amplitude of $s'_2(t)$, guaranteeing that both signals yield the exact same positive area over the duration $T_b$.

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{E} &= \int_{0}^{T_b} [s'_1(t)]^2 dt = \int_{0}^{T_b} [s'_2(t)]^2 dt \\[15pt] &= \int_{0}^{T_b} \left( \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}} \right)^2 dt \\[15pt] &= \int_{0}^{T_b} \frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b} dt = \frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b} [T_b - 0] = \mathcal{E}_b \end{aligned} $$
Part 3: Vector Coordinates
  • Project signals onto basis functions.
  • $\psi_1(t)$ isolates the first half ($0 \to T_b/2$).
  • $\psi_2(t)$ isolates the second half ($T_b/2 \to T_b$).

(Reminder: We are substituting the specific $\psi_1(t)$ and $\psi_2(t)$ given by the problem statement. We did not calculate these using Gram-Schmidt!)

Coordinates for $s'_1$: $$ \begin{aligned} s'_{11} &= \int_{0}^{T_b/2} s'_1(t)\psi_1(t) dt \\ &= \int_{0}^{T_b/2} \left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_b}}\right) dt = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}} \end{aligned} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} s'_{12} &= \int_{T_b/2}^{T_b} s'_1(t)\psi_2(t) dt \\ &= \int_{T_b/2}^{T_b} \left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_b}}\right) dt = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}} \end{aligned} $$
Coordinates for $s'_2$: $$ \begin{aligned} s'_{21} &= \int_{0}^{T_b/2} s'_2(t)\psi_1(t) dt \\ &= \int_{0}^{T_b/2} \left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_b}}\right) dt = \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}} \end{aligned} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} s'_{22} &= \int_{T_b/2}^{T_b} s'_2(t)\psi_2(t) dt \\ &= \int_{T_b/2}^{T_b} \left(-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{T_b}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{T_b}}\right) dt = -\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}} \end{aligned} $$
Final Vectors:
$s'_1 = \left( \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}}, \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}} \right)$ $s'_2 = \left( \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}}, -\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{E}_b}{2}} \right)$

Geometric Visualization of the Vectors

The vectors are positioned precisely in the First and Fourth quadrants. Their geometric angle is exactly 90 degrees (Orthogonal), and their distance from the origin is identical ($\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_b}$).
